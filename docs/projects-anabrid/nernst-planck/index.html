Projects@anabrid · ion transport with a charge-conservation constraint

Ion transport on an analog computer

Two ion species diffuse and drift in an electric field that they create themselves. The field is fixed by a constraint, not by a differential equation in time. This project solves that system on the REDAC analog computer and measures it against solvers on a central processing unit and a graphics processing unit.

Dissolve a salt in water and let the concentration differ from place to place. The positive and the negative ion both diffuse. They do not diffuse at the same speed, so one runs ahead of the other and an electric field builds up that holds them together. The Nernst-Planck system describes this. It couples the transport of each species to a potential, and the potential is not stepped in time. It is whatever makes charge conservation hold at every instant.

Why this is hard on any computer

A system with a rule that must hold at every instant, next to equations that evolve in time, is a differential-algebraic system. A digital solver treats the rule as a linear system to be solved at every step. An analog computer has no step and no linear solver. It has integrators, multipliers and wires. So the question is how to write the rule into a circuit, and what the circuit costs once its coefficients have to fit the converters of a real machine.

What we do

We build the circuit in more than one way, from the one that removes the constraint by hand to the one that enforces it with a fast relaxation. We run each against a digital reference of the same problem, so that every discrepancy has one owner: the discretization, the relaxation, or the hardware. The same problem is also run on a central processing unit (CPU) and on a graphics processing unit (GPU), to put the analog result next to what a digital solver spends.

What it does not offer

It is not a claim of speed. Whether a circuit of this kind can beat a digital solver on this problem is the open question, and the work is built to answer it either way. The statements here are bound to one spatial dimension, to a salt with two ion species, and to the REDAC installation we work with.

Behind the glass: a bench in the browser where the reader moves the diffusivity ratio, the grid size and the stiffness of the constraint, the reasoning behind the circuit, and the numbers, including where the method falls short. The work is unpublished, hence the password.
