Oscillatory computing · interactive demonstrators

GNSS-independent localization and timing

Coupled oscillators for GNSS-independent timing, spoof detection and passive direction finding. Two interactive benches, by password.

Positioning, navigation and timing hang on one thread, and the thread is GNSS. Cut it and a surprising amount stops working; fake it and the failure hides, because the receiver keeps producing a position and a time that are wrong and look fine. This project asks what a network of coupled oscillators can do about that, using the same self-organized synchronization that runs through the rest of the research.

Two capabilities come out of one kind of hardware. A network of base stations (BS), each a phase-locked loop, settles into a shared reference clock with no master and without knowing the delays between them. Because that reference never reads GNSS, a local receiver (LR) can catch a spoofing carry-off simply by watching its GNSS clock drift away from the network consensus. The same coupled loops, a baseline apart, hear the direction of an incoming wavefront from the tiny difference in its arrival time, so a few bearings to known base stations place the receiver, or, run in reverse, place a hostile emitter.

Behind the glass: the full write-up with the equations, the honest limits, and two interactive benches, one for the spoof detector and one for the direction finder, labelled in base-station and local-receiver terms throughout.

Two live benches: spoof detection and direction finding

A network of coupled clocks that holds true time without GNSS and catches a spoofing carry-off as a divergence from its own consensus, and a pair of coupled loops that hears the direction of a base station well enough to place a receiver or a hostile emitter. Both run in your browser, with base-station and local-receiver labels throughout. Unpublished, so behind a password.
