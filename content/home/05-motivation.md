Curiosity asks *what if*. Innovation begins when I ask *how do we get it done*.

My sweet spot is the bridge between basic science and real-world application. My field is
nonlinear dynamics, specifically what happens when many oscillators are coupled and the
signals between them arrive late. For years that was a blackboard problem. Then we simplified
the math. This made the core problems tractable. Subsequently, in our experiments, 24 GHz
oscillators coupled over a distance of half a kilometre synchronized successfully. The
experiments showed that our model predicts the dynamics of networks of delay-coupled
oscillators qualitatively and quantitatively. So we [patented](#patents) this novel approach:
how to design the properties of such systems so that they reliably reach different types of
self-organized synchronization.

As Chief Scientific Officer I am responsible for the scientific direction of anabrid GmbH.
I lead our hardware development. And I lead the technical conversation with our customers. The REDAC, the largest
reconfigurable discrete analog computer in operation, was designed, built and commissioned by
the team I lead, and handed over to the DLR in 2025. It now carries four pilot projects in the
[DLR Quantum Computing Initiative](https://qci.dlr.de/), and research beyond DLR at TU Dresden,
Hochschule Karlsruhe and other institutes.

Before industry I spent fifteen years at the Max Planck Institute for the Physics of Complex
Systems. I directed the VIP+ validation project on phase-locked loop synchronization together
with Fraunhofer IZM and TU Dresden. It proved that high-level theory survives the rigours of a
real application. I also learned there that the strongest results do not come out of silos.
They come from interdisciplinary teams, a real market need, and a clear view of where you are
going.

When it comes to leadership, my time spent in professional sports influences how I lead
today. I played American football with the Dresden
Monarchs, and it taught me that a perfect playbook is worth nothing without execution,
discipline, and a team that trusts each other under pressure. I run research and development
the same way. My job is to keep a culture where any question can be asked and where new ideas
still have to meet a performance bar.
