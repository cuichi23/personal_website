A mutually synchronized network has no reference clock and therefore no single point of
failure. Real infrastructure still has to be steered onto an external time standard, so we
asked what happens when a reference is injected into one node only. Three 24 GHz oscillators
were coupled in a ring and in a chain, forced by an external oscillator, and the range of
reference frequencies the network still follows was measured. The measured entrainment
ranges agree with the nonlinear model, which means a flat-hierarchy network can be locked to
an outside standard without giving up the robustness that made it attractive.
